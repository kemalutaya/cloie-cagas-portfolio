Proof of Work

Samples clients can actually inspect

Three artifacts from live campaigns. I painted out client names, addresses, and phone numbers. Everything else is exactly how it runs, and each one comes with why I built it that way.

CRM pipeline board

GoHighLevel Opportunities board for a Texas seller-lead campaign: 70 open opportunities moving across Offer Made, Long-Term Nurture, 30 Day Nurture, and Contract Sent, with call, SMS, note, and task activity logged on every card.

Why it is built this way

The stages are named after the seller’s decision, not after my activity. Offer Made and Contract Sent are the two lanes where somebody owes somebody an answer today. Everyone who said not yet goes into a nurture lane, sorted by how long they asked me to wait. That sorting is the part that matters. A lead who goes quiet stops padding the active pipeline, and it still comes back on a date instead of dying quietly in a list. Every card carries its own call, SMS, note, and task counts with the source attached, so an acquisition manager can open one cold and see what already happened without messaging me first. I keep the board filtered to one market at a time. A board you have to scroll sideways to read is a board nobody opens twice.

Appointment tracker

The live call sheet behind the campaign: offer claimed, lead condition, and the prospect’s own notes per record, then Called, Booked, Showed, Package, and Sales checkpoints, with branch and booking date for every set appointment.

Why it is built this way

The columns run in the order the call actually happens, so filling it in takes no thinking while someone is still talking. The five checkpoints stay separate on purpose. Collapse them into one status field and you lose the number that tells you whether a campaign is working, which is the drop between Booked and Showed. Colour does the reporting for me: green moved forward, red stalled. Whoever opens the sheet reads outcomes before they read a single word. The notes column keeps the prospect’s own phrasing and the time they asked to be called back, and that is what makes the confirmation call sound like a continuation instead of a second cold call.

Cold call script

The seller-lead opener as it is actually run: intro and reason for the call, property condition, then price, timeline, and the loan-balance question that qualifies the deal.

Why it is built this way

The whole thing is built for the first ten seconds. Name, reason for the call, then a question that gives them an easy way out: were you still looking to sell that one? That is where a seller decides whether to stay on the line. I introduce myself as the owner’s assistant rather than the decision maker on purpose. It lowers the pressure, it keeps the closer’s leverage where it belongs, and booking the appointment becomes the obvious next step instead of a negotiation I was never there to have. The condition questions stay light and factual, roof, HVAC, repairs, recent updates, because they are easy to answer and they get the seller talking. Price and timeline come next, since those two decide whether the lead is worth an acquisition manager’s hour. The mortgage question goes last, softened and announced as the last one. It is the highest friction question on the page, and it only gets an honest answer once the rest of the call has done its work.
